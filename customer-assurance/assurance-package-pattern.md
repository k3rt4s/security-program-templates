# Customer assurance package

A working pattern for the material a security team gives prospective customers, opinionated about the fact that every answer is a binding commitment rather than a marketing statement.

Security questionnaires consume an enormous amount of security-team time, and the standard advice is to answer once and reuse. That advice is correct and it is not the interesting part. The interesting part is that the reused answer is a durable commitment: it constrains what the organization may later do, it is quoted back after an incident, and it is generated by whoever is under the most deal pressure.

## What this is, and what it is not

It is the curated set of material handed to a prospect or customer to answer security due diligence: what is in it, where each item comes from, and what happens after an answer is given.

It is not a questionnaire response service, not a trust-center product comparison, and not a sales asset. The tooling in this space is abundant and changes yearly; the durable question is what you are willing to commit to in writing, which no tool decides for you.

## Every answer is a commitment

The reframe that makes this a security artifact rather than a sales one.

An answer given in a questionnaire is a representation about how the organization operates. It gets attached to a contract, cited in a schedule, retained by the customer indefinitely, and read back during an incident or an audit. It typically outlives both the person who wrote it and the control it described.

Three consequences follow, and each is uncomfortable in a live deal:

**A control you have committed to is not available for exception.** This is already stated in the [exception record](../risk-management/security-exception-record.md): an exception that makes an existing statement untrue is a legal question rather than a risk question. The assurance package is where most of those statements are created, usually faster than the exception process can absorb them.

**Aspirational answers are the expensive kind.** "Yes, with a project underway" is read by the customer as "yes." When the project slips, the gap is not a roadmap item, it is a discrepancy between a written representation and reality. The honest answer, that the control is not in place and here is the compensating measure and the date, closes fewer deals in the moment and creates no liability.

**The commitment outlives the deal.** Answers given for one customer become the template answered for the next hundred, because nobody re-derives them. One optimistic answer propagates silently.

## Generate from evidence, do not author

The structural fix, and it changes who does the work.

Answers written by the team under deal pressure drift toward yes. Answers generated from artifacts the program already maintains cannot, because the artifact is the constraint.

| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Which frameworks, which scope, what evidence   | [Control mapping](../compliance/control-framework-mapping-pattern.md), including the coverage qualifiers                                                                                                 |

Where a question has no source artifact, that is the finding. It means the organization is about to commit in writing to something it does not track internally, and the correct response is to build the tracking or decline the commitment, not to answer from memory.

## The package

Four tiers, released by what the relationship justifies. Releasing everything to everyone is how competitive detail and architectural specifics reach people with no obligation to protect them.

- **Public.** Certifications held with their scope, a plain description of the security program, subprocessor list, and vulnerability disclosure contact. No NDA. Answers the majority of early questions and deflects the majority of questionnaires.
- **Under NDA.** Audit report, penetration test summary, architecture overview, standard questionnaire response set.
- **Negotiated.** Custom questionnaire responses, controls evidence, and audit-right terms. These consume real time, so route them by deal size deliberately rather than by whoever asks loudest.
- **Never.** Raw scan output, unredacted penetration test detail, internal risk register, exception register. A prospect asking for these is asking you to demonstrate that you would hand the same material to anyone.

Publish the tiers internally so sales knows the shape before promising anything. Most escalations come from a commitment made before security was consulted.

## The review gate

One question decides whether an answer ships: would you be comfortable with this sentence read aloud, unedited, during an incident postmortem eighteen months from now.

It is a better filter than any accuracy checklist because it catches the two failure modes at once. Aspirational answers fail it immediately. So do answers that are literally true but constructed to mislead, which are the ones that do real damage to credibility.

Every answer set carries a version and a date, and the [decision rights register](../governance/decision-rights-register.md) names who may approve a non-standard answer. That authority is exercised under deal pressure, so it should sit with someone whose incentives are not the deal.

## Maintain it or retire it

An assurance package is a set of representations that decay. Stale material is worse than none, because it is being actively relied upon.

Re-derive from source artifacts on a fixed cycle, and immediately whenever the underlying artifact changes: a certification lapses or its scope changes, a subprocessor is added, an exception is granted over a control the package claims, or a control named in the package fails a test.

That third trigger is the one that gets missed, and it is the reason this document belongs in a security repository rather than a sales one. An exception granted internally can silently falsify a public commitment. Reconciling the exception register against the assurance package is a short exercise that almost nobody runs.

## Where this connects

- Controls claimed here are not available for exception. See [exception record](../risk-management/security-exception-record.md), which routes those to counsel.
- Voluntary and contractual commitments made here are rows in the [regulatory applicability register](../compliance/regulatory-applicability-register.md). That register is what makes the non-exceptable list knowable rather than remembered.
- Answers derive from the [control mapping](../compliance/control-framework-mapping-pattern.md) and inherit its coverage qualifiers. An answer more confident than its mapping is a defect in the answer.
- Time spent on questionnaires, and deflection rate from the public tier, are candidate indicators for the [metrics catalog](../metrics/security-metrics-catalog.md), with the caveat that deflection rate is gamed by narrowing what counts as a questionnaire.
- Authority to approve a non-standard answer is a row in the [decision rights register](../governance/decision-rights-register.md).

## Common failure modes

1. **Answers authored rather than generated.** Written under deal pressure by people whose incentive is yes, then reused forever.
2. **Aspirational yes.** "Yes, with a project underway" is recorded as yes and becomes a discrepancy when the project slips.
3. **No source artifact.** A question is answered from memory, which means committing in writing to something nobody tracks.
4. **One tier for everyone.** Sensitive material reaches parties with no obligation to protect it, because there was no released-by-tier rule.
5. **Commitments invisible to the exception process.** An exception is granted over a control the package claims, and nothing reconciles the two.
6. **Stale and still circulating.** The package is not re-derived when its sources change, so customers rely on representations that stopped being true.
7. **Approval sitting with the deal owner.** The person who benefits from the optimistic answer is the person who approves it.
